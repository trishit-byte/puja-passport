# Durga Puja Cultural Research: *Anandapur: Pujo Nights*

Research brief for a cozy browser game set on **Ashtami night** in **Anandapur**, a fictional Bengali city. The player visits 8 pandals and collects passport stamps. This document covers the real traditions the game draws on, the Bengali language and naming, art direction, and a checklist for cultural authenticity.

## 1. History, meaning, and UNESCO recognition

- **What it is.** Durga Puja is a ten-day worship of the Hindu mother goddess Durga, held each September or October. It is centred on Kolkata and celebrated across India and by the Bengali diaspora. In UNESCO's description, rituals begin on **Mahalaya**, "when eyes are painted onto the clay images to bring the goddess to life," and end when the images are immersed in the river their clay came from. ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/durga-puja-in-kolkata-00703))
- **UNESCO 2021.** "Durga Puja in Kolkata" was inscribed on the **Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** in 2021 at the 16th session of the Intergovernmental Committee (decision 16.COM 8.b.15, file 00703). UNESCO says that during the festival "the divides of class, religion and ethnicities collapse as crowds of spectators walk around to admire the installations," and calls the festival "a thriving ground for collaborative artists and designers." ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/durga-puja-in-kolkata-00703))
- **Meaning.** The festival marks Durga's victory over the buffalo demon **Mahishasura**, or "the victory of good over evil." It is also a **homecoming**: Durga visits her parental (earthly) home with her four children. ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja)) Bengali Ramayana versions tell of Rama worshipping Durga out of season, in autumn, before fighting Ravana. This is why the autumn puja is linked with *akal bodhon* ("untimely awakening"). ([Wikipedia: Durga Puja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))
- **Deep history.** Durga's iconography was already established in Pala-Sena era carvings (around the 12th century). The *Devi Mahatmya*, recited during the puja, is dated to roughly 400–600 CE. Royal and landed families sponsored public pujas from at least the 16th century. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) Incredible India gives **1610** as the first recorded Durga Puja in Kolkata. ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja))
- **Mahalaya radio.** Since the 1950s, many Bengali Hindus have woken around 4 a.m. on Mahalaya to hear the All India Radio broadcast *Mahishasuramardini*. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))
- **Scale.** A 2019 British Council study valued the creative economy around Durga Puja in West Bengal at **₹32,377 crore**, about 2.6% of state GDP. Idol makers, dhakis, and priests depend on the season for much of their yearly income. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))
- **2026 dates.** Incredible India lists the main days as **17–21 October 2026** (Shashthi to Dashami), so Ashtami falls around 19 October. ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja)) Exact dates and times (*tithi*) follow the Bengali almanac (*panjika*) and change every year.

## 2. Durga, Mahishasura, and the standard idol tableau

**Story.** Mahishasura, a shape-shifting buffalo demon, could not be beaten by any god. The gods pooled their powers to create Durga, who rides a lion and kills him with her trident (*trishul*). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))

**The standard Bengali tableau (*protima*)**, read left to right as you face it. Families and committees vary in the details. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))

| Figure | Where | Visual cues |
|---|---|---|
| **Durga** | Centre, raised | Ten arms holding weapons, three eyes, calm face despite the battle. Spears the demon with her trident. |
| **Lion** (her *vahana*) | Under or beside Durga | Attacking Mahishasura. In Bengal it often looks horse-like or stylised. |
| **Mahishasura** | Lower centre | Bursting out of a severed buffalo. Green or dark skin, sword and shield, a snake binding him. |
| **Lakshmi** | Flanking Durga (usually on her right, viewer's left) | Wealth. Pink or golden, lotus, owl (*pecha*). |
| **Ganesh** | Outer position, Lakshmi's side | Elephant head, mouse. The **Kola Bou** stands next to him. |
| **Saraswati** | Other flank | Knowledge. White, *veena*, swan. |
| **Kartik** | Outer position, Saraswati's side | Handsome young warrior. Peacock, bow. |
| **Chalchitra** | Arched backdrop | Painted half-dome behind the figures (see below). |

- **Kola Bou / Nabapatrika.** Nine plants (banana, colocasia, turmeric, and others) are bundled together, and the banana plant is dressed in a red-bordered sari. This is the **Kola Bou** ("banana bride"), placed beside Ganesh after a ritual bath at dawn on **Saptami**. Popular belief calls her Ganesh's wife. Many sources and devotees reject that reading and treat her as a form of Durga. *(summary of [IndiaCurrents](https://indiacurrents.com/the-mysterious-nabapatrika-kola-bou-at-the-heart-of-durga-puja/), [Hindu Blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2007/10/kalabau-on-maha-saptami-of-durga-puja.html?hl=en))* **Game note:** call her "Kola Bou" or "Nabapatrika". Do not label her as "Ganesh's wife".
- **Ekchala vs. separate figures.** In the traditional **ekchala** ("one roof/frame") style, all five deities and the demon share one arched frame. Shobhabazar Rajbari is cited as an example. *(summary of [Outlook Traveller: Bonedi Bari](https://www.outlookindia.com/traveller/destinations/top-10-bonedi-bari-durga-puja/))* Sahapedia describes **ek chala** (single backdrop) and later **do chala** styles, plus named idol types such as Khas Bangla (decorated entirely with sola) and Art Bangla. ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/kolkatas-potters-colony-kumortuli)) Many modern theme pandals split the figures into separate frames.
- **Chalchitra.** A Bengal *patachitra* painted on the idol's backdrop (*chal*). Cloth is stretched over a half-dome bamboo frame, coated with clay and chalk, then painted with natural pigments. Common motifs are Shiva and Parvati, Kali, Mahishamardini, and the ten avatars of Vishnu. The Markini shape is the most common. ([Wikipedia: Chalchitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalchitra))
- **Adornment: *sholar saaj* vs. *daaker saaj*.** In *sholar saaj* the idol is decorated with white **sholapith**, the pith of a marsh reed, carved into crowns, garlands, and filigree. In *daaker saaj* it is decorated with silver foil and sequins. One popular etymology says the foil once came "by post" (*daak*) from Germany. Treat this as folk etymology. *(summary of [SheThePeople](https://www.shethepeople.tv/author-spotlight/guest-contributions/daaker-shaaj-durga-puja-10468488), [Tanishq blog](https://blog.tanishq.co.in/how-bengals-sacred-crafts-inspire-modern-jewellery/))*

## 3. Clay idol making and Kumortuli

- **Kumortuli** is the potters' quarter of North Kolkata, between Rabindra Sarani (Chitpur Road) and the Hooghly. The potters began with pots and moved into making images for mansions and community pujas. ([Wikipedia: Kumortuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumortuli)) Around **1757**, Raja Nabakrishna Deb of Shobhabazar brought skilled *kumors* from Krishnanagar for his family puja, and this is taken as the quarter's origin. ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/kolkatas-potters-colony-kumortuli))
- **Process** ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/kolkatas-potters-colony-kumortuli)):
  1. **Kathamo:** a skeleton of bamboo and wood, wrapped and tied with **straw**.
  2. **Entel mati:** sticky clay mixed with rice husk, as the first layer.
  3. **Bele mati:** finer clay for the second layer and the face, polished smooth.
  4. Faces, palms, and fingers are often cast separately from moulds.
  5. Painting, dressing, and ornaments, then **chokkhudaan** (চক্ষুদান, "gift of eyes"), done by a specialist.
- **Where the clay comes from.** UNESCO says "unfired clay pulled from the Ganga River" ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/durga-puja-in-kolkata-00703)). Sahapedia says the riverside clay comes from Uluberia because it has less sand than the Hooghly banks ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/kolkatas-potters-colony-kumortuli)). Both are riverine: the Hooghly is a channel of the Ganga. In the game, "river clay" or *Ganga mati* is a fair simplification. Some Kolkata traditions also add soil from "forbidden territories" (popularly, the courtesans' quarter). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))
- **Atmosphere for art.** Studios are packed into narrow lanes and open directly onto them. The quarter smells of wet mud, half-finished idols dry along the alleys, and the light inside is often dim. The season runs July to January and peaks August to October. ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/kolkatas-potters-colony-kumortuli))
- **Environment.** Lead and other heavy-metal paints washing off idols during immersion polluted rivers, and West Bengal has banned hazardous paints. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) This is a natural hook for the fictional "Shobuj Swapno" eco-pandal.

## 4. Bonedi bari pujas (aristocratic household pujas)

- *Bonedi bari* means old, established family homes. Their pujas are held in the house's **thakur dalan**, a pillared, arched worship hall opening onto an inner courtyard. **Shobhabazar Rajbari (1757)** is the most-cited example. Raja Nabakrishna Deb entertained Robert Clive and East India Company officers there. The puja keeps a traditional ekchala image, and Swami Vivekananda was later given a civic reception in the same courtyard. *(summary of [Wikipedia: Shobhabazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shobhabazar), [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlookindia.com/traveller/destinations/top-10-bonedi-bari-durga-puja/))*
- These pujas feel quieter and more ritual-centred than theme pandals: family members in traditional dress, inherited customs (Shobhabazar offers sweets in place of cooked rice), and old furniture and chandeliers. They suit the cozy tone well.

## 5. Barowari and sarbojanin (community) pujas

- **Barowari** pujas are paid for by public subscription and run by a committee. The usual origin story is **Guptipara (Hooghly)**: men barred from a zamindar's household puja organised their own. Sources disagree on whether this was **1761 or 1790** and whether there were seven or twelve founders. The folk etymology *baro* ("twelve") + *yari* ("friends") is popular, but Wikipedia gives a different derivation. ([Wikipedia: Barowari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barowari))
- **Sarbojanin** (সর্বজনীন, "for everyone") is the later term. Sahapedia dates the first community puja in Kolkata to **1910** at Balaram Basu Ghat Road ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/kolkatas-potters-colony-kumortuli)). Other sources cite the Nebubagan/Baghbazar puja of **1919**. ([Wikipedia: Barowari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barowari))
- **Today:** local clubs and youth groups raise donations (*chanda*), build the pandal, and staff it with volunteers. Large pandals often have corporate sponsors. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))

## 6. Theme pandals and pandal hopping

- **Pandals** are temporary pavilions of bamboo and cloth, increasingly built as full artistic installations around a **theme**. Past themes include folk culture, cinema, womanhood, the environment, and queer and transgender marginalisation. Awards recognise idol design, sustainability, and other categories. ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) The competition has pushed up costs, as with an 88-foot Durga in Kolkata in 2015. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja))
- **Pandal hopping** (*thakur dekha*, "seeing the goddess") is the walk from pandal to pandal, done by young and old alike, often late into the night and sometimes until dawn. Crowds in festive clothes fill the streets while the dhak plays. ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) The game's passport-stamp loop is a playful stand-in for this real practice.

## 7. The five main days

| Day | Bengali | Key practices |
|---|---|---|
| **Shashthi** (6th) | ষষ্ঠী | *Bodhon* (awakening) and welcoming the goddess. Faces are unveiled, and the festivities begin. |
| **Saptami** (7th) | সপ্তমী | Dawn bath of the **Nabapatrika/Kola Bou** in the river, then installation beside Ganesh. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) |
| **Ashtami** (8th) | অষ্টমী | **Pushpanjali/anjali** in the morning: devotees, often fasting, offer flowers and bel leaves while repeating mantras after the priest. **Kumari Puja**: a young girl is worshipped as the goddess, a tradition Swami Vivekananda began at Belur Math in 1901 *(summary of [Belur Math media](https://media.belurmath.org/durga-puja-2021-ashtami-and-kumari-puja-7712/) and related reports)*. **Sandhi Puja**: held at the junction of Ashtami and Navami, traditionally the last 24 minutes of one and the first 24 of the other, with **108 lotuses and 108 lamps**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) Evening aarti and **dhunuchi naach**. ([30Stades](https://30stades.com/art-culture/dhunuchi-dance-with-incense-smoke-devotion-durga-puja-west-bengal-1566249)) |
| **Navami** (9th) | নবমী | *Homa* (fire offering) and *bhog* (offered food, often khichuri). Some places hold Kumari Puja on this day. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja)) |
| **Bijoya Dashami** (10th) | বিজয়া দশমী | **Sindur khela**: women smear vermilion on the goddess and on each other. **Bisorjon** (immersion): idols are carried in procession and immersed in a river. Sweets are shared and people visit each other with "Shubho Bijoya." ([Wikipedia: Sindoor Khela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindoor_Khela), [Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja)) |

**What this means for Ashtami night:**
- Morning *anjali* and Kumari Puja are **already over** by night. NPCs can mention them ("I did anjali this morning, fasted till noon!"), but they should not be shown happening.
- **Sandhi Puja** may fall at night in some years, depending on the almanac. It can appear as a distant bell, drum, and lamps moment, but not as player-performed ritual.
- **Evening aarti, dhak, and dhunuchi naach** fit Ashtami night well.
- **Sindur khela and bisorjon belong to Dashami.** Do not show them. Sindur khela has also been criticised as patriarchal because traditionally only married women took part; recent campaigns invite all women. ([Wikipedia: Sindoor Khela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindoor_Khela)) That is one more reason to leave it out.

## 8. Sound: dhak, dhakis, kansor

- The **dhak** is a large barrel drum slung from the neck and played with two thin sticks. Dhakis traditionally decorate the top of the drum with a tall plume of white feathers. Dhakis are often hereditary drummers from rural Bengal: Bankura, Bardhaman, Malda, Murshidabad, Nadia, Birbhum. Before the puja they gather at **Sealdah station** to be hired by committees. Their income has been squeezed, and some committees have switched to recorded music. ([PARI](https://ruralindiaonline.org/article/the-down-but-not-out-dhakis-of-the-durga-puja)) Incredible India: "the city echoes with the enchanting beats of the traditional Bengali drum, the 'dhaak.'" ([Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/durga-puja))
- The **kansor** (কাঁসর) is a small bell-metal gong struck with a stick. It often goes with the dhak and the conch (*shankha*) at aarti. *Not confirmed by a fetched source; this comes from general knowledge.*
- **Audio palette:** dhak plus kansor, the conch, hand bells (*ghonta*), the crowd, distant loudspeaker announcements, and a priest chanting in the distance. Avoid looping a single sacred mantra as background music.

## 9. Dhunuchi naach

The **dhunuchi** is a flared clay incense burner on a stem. It is filled with **coconut husk, camphor, and *dhuno*** (sal tree resin). People dance with it to the dhak at the evening aarti, mainly on **Ashtami** and on Dashami as the idol leaves for immersion. Skilled dancers hold up to three: one in each hand and one in the teeth. Men once danced alone; women now dance prominently too. ([30Stades](https://30stades.com/art-culture/dhunuchi-dance-with-incense-smoke-devotion-durga-puja-west-bengal-1566249)) **Visual:** thick white fragrant smoke, glowing embers, and figures swaying in front of the idol.

## 10. Alpona

**Alpona** (আলপনা) is free-hand floor art in white rice-flour paste, drawn with the fingers or a cloth. Motifs include lotuses, conch shells, creepers, paddy sheaves, Lakshmi's footprints, and owls. Circular designs can serve as a base for an idol. Alpona is a major part of Durga Puja, with competitions and street-long designs painted by volunteers. Teaching at Visva-Bharati's Kala Bhavana helped formalise it as an art. ([Wikipedia: Alpana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpana)) **Game use:** alpona fits naturally on courtyard floors, pandal entrances, and stamp borders.

## 11. Lighting and the Chandannagar tradition

**Chandannagar** (Hooghly district) is famous for its lighting artisans. The craft grew out of the town's **Jagaddhatri Puja**, which is a different festival from Durga Puja. It moved from gas lamps to string lights and now to LEDs, programmable pixels, and animated light boards, and Chandannagar crews light pujas across India. Sridhar Das is often named as a pioneer. *(summary of [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/west-bengal-chandannagar-jagaddhatri-puja-lighting-puja-pandal-enn24110901801), [News Arena](https://newsarenaindia.com/lifestyle/chandannagar-the-glowing-heartbeat-of-bengal/57589))* **Visual:** street-long arches and gates of tiny bulbs, animated light panels (peacocks, birds, mythological scenes, everyday life), and warm-white fairy lights against the night sky. This is the direct reference for "Alo'r Utsav".

## 12. Food: street food, mishti, cha

- **Phuchka** (ফুচকা): hollow fried shells filled with spiced potato and dunked in tamarind water. Vendors set up after about 4 p.m. near the big pandals. **Churmur** is the crushed "cousin". **Kathi rolls**: paratha wrapped around kebab with onion and chilli. **Telebhaja/chop** (fritters) eaten with muri. **Cha in clay cups** at roadside stalls, with biscuits. Also kochuri with alur dom, biryani, and Chinese-Bengali chowmein. ([Outlook Traveller: street food](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/experiences/food-and-drink/in-kolkata-for-durga-puja-here-are-some-must-have-street-foods))
- **Jhalmuri** (ঝালমুড়ি): puffed rice tossed with mustard oil, chanachur, onion, chilli, cucumber, and coriander, served in **paper cones**. ([Wikipedia: Jhalmuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhalmuri))
- **Moglai porota** (মোগলাই পরোটা): a paratha stuffed with egg (and sometimes minced meat) and pan-fried. It is a Kolkata staple; Outlook mentions it only in passing. *(Description from general knowledge.)*
- **Matir bhNar** (মাটির ভাঁড়): an unglazed, single-use clay cup for cha. Tea soaks into the walls and takes on an earthy smell. ([Wikipedia: Kulhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulhar))
- **Mishti:** **roshogolla**, chhena balls in light syrup. West Bengal received the GI tag "Banglar Rasogolla" in 2017; the origin is disputed with Odisha. ([Wikipedia: Rasgulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasgulla)) **Sondesh** is a dry chhena sweet pressed in moulds such as conch shells and fish. **Mishti doi** is sweetened, caramel-coloured yoghurt set in clay pots. *(Sondesh and mishti doi descriptions are general knowledge; both appear in Wikipedia's Bengal cuisine navigation.)*
- **Bhog:** khichuri, labra (mixed vegetables), and payesh are served at many pujas. In the game, frame it as being offered prasad by a volunteer, not as "buying" it.

## 13. Volunteers, queues, community

Pandals are run by local committees and volunteers. Typical sights: volunteers in matching club T-shirts or kurtas with ID badges, rope barricades and zig-zag bamboo queues at big pandals, separate entry and exit lanes, megaphones and PA announcements ("lost child at the help desk"), police kiosks, first-aid tents, and volunteers handing out water. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja), [Wikipedia: Barowari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barowari)) The UNESCO framing, crowds of all backgrounds admiring the installations side by side, is a good tonal anchor. ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/durga-puja-in-kolkata-00703))

## 16. FACT vs. FICTION

| Real tradition (FACT) | Our invention (FICTION) |
|---|---|
| Durga Puja, its days, rituals, iconography, and crafts (Kumortuli, chalchitra, sholapith, alpona, dhak, dhunuchi) | **Anandapur**, a fictional city that blends Kolkata and small-town Bengal |
| Bonedi bari, barowari/sarbojanin, and theme pandals as *types* | The **8 named pandals** and **5 districts**. No real committee or family is depicted. |
| Pandal hopping late into the night | The **passport and stamps** mechanic. Real pandals do not stamp passports; it is a game device for exploration. |
| Chandannagar lighting craft | Specific light installations in Anandapur |
| Street food, cha in bhNar, mishti | Any prices, vendor characters, or recipes shown in-game |
| Ashtami as a real day with real rites | The compressed one-night timeline |
| Dashami **bisorjon** (immersion) is the real festival close | The **sunrise finale** is an invented ending: dawn after a night of pandal hopping, tea at the ghat, the city waking. It is **not** an immersion, and no idol should be carried to or put into the water. It is the next morning (Navami), not the end of the festival. |

In-game credits or an "About" screen should say: *"Anandapur and its pandals are fictional. Durga Puja is a living tradition inscribed by UNESCO in 2021; this game is a loving tribute, not a depiction of real worship."*

## 17. Cultural authenticity checklist (for devs)

**Idol and worship**
- [ ] Depict the idol respectfully: static, well lit, and centred. **No clicking on, rotating, picking up, collecting, or "unlocking" the idol**, and no idol as a toy, inventory item, or collectible figurine.
- [ ] Stamps show the **pandal or its theme** (architecture, lights, a motif), **not** the goddess's face.
- [ ] Do not gamify worship. No points or achievements for praying, doing anjali, or "completing" aarti. Players can watch rituals; they do not score them.
- [ ] The player does not perform priestly roles (aarti, Sandhi Puja, Kumari Puja).
- [ ] Mahishasura stays a mythological figure. Do not restyle him as a cartoon "boss".
- [ ] A **temple-style bell** at a pandal entrance can be visitor-accessible (people really do ring them). Treat it as an ambient touch with a soft chime, not a spam-click toy or a reward trigger.
- [ ] Shoes: you can't enforce removing them, and shouldn't make it a mechanic. Optionally show a shoe-rack area and barefoot NPCs near the idol platform as set dressing.

**Space and objects**
- [ ] **No walking on or over the alpona in the sacred area** in front of the idol. Use colliders and paths so the player can't, and route the camera around it. Decorative alpona on side walkways is fine to walk beside.
- [ ] The player does not cross the barrier onto the idol platform (*bedi*).
- [ ] Do not let the player pick up, throw, or knock over offering plates, lamps, conches, flowers, or the Kola Bou.

**Timeline accuracy (Ashtami night)**
- [ ] **No sindur khela** and no immersion. Both are Dashami.
- [ ] Do not use "Shubho Bijoya" or "Ashchhe bochhor abar hobe" as active greetings tonight. NPCs may *anticipate* Dashami ("Only two more days…").
- [ ] Morning anjali and Kumari Puja are referred to in the past tense.
- [ ] Evening aarti, dhak, and dhunuchi naach are all appropriate.

**Music and interaction**
- [ ] Frame any **dhak rhythm interaction** as appreciating, listening to, or keeping time with the dhaki, e.g. "tap along" or "learn the bol". The dhaki is a respected artist, not a jukebox. No fail state that boos the player.
- [ ] Do not loop sacred mantras as background music. Keep chanting distant and ambient.
- [ ] Dhunuchi dancers are NPCs; the player watches or claps.

**People and tone**
- [ ] No mockery of priests, dhakis, vendors, or devotees. No "funny Indian accent" voice lines.
- [ ] Show diverse visitors (ages, genders, faiths, abilities, families, friend groups, grandparents with walking sticks), in line with UNESCO's emphasis on inclusivity.
- [ ] No alcohol at pandals.
- [ ] Pay dhakis and vendors fairly in any in-game economy. Don't treat them as quest dispensers with no inner life.
- [ ] No real brand logos or real puja committee names.

**Language**
- [ ] Have a native Bengali speaker review every string, especially the ⚠ items.
- [ ] Use a font with proper conjunct shaping (see §14).

### Architecture motifs
- **Thakur dalan:** a raised, pillared hall with **multi-foliate (scalloped) arches** facing an open **courtyard (*uthon*)**. Corinthian-style columns, stucco mouldings, chequered black-and-white marble floors, hanging glass chandeliers, and an upper gallery behind wooden lattice. Use for Bonedi Bari Puja and Bonedi Para.
- **North Kolkata street houses:** two or three storeys with tall shuttered windows painted green (louvred) and **wrought-iron and cast-iron balconies** with floral grilles. Add red oxide floors, rooftop terraces (*chhad*) with water tanks and drying saris, and narrow lanes with hanging wires.
- **Pandal construction:** bamboo scaffolding wrapped in printed cloth, plywood, and thermocol. Facades can mimic temples, palaces, or abstract theme art. Add a gate (*toran*) with Bengali signage, a light arch tunnel leading to it, and the idol platform under a canopy.
- **Bengal temple styles** for Gram Bangla: *chala* curved roofs (*do-chala*, *aat-chala*), terracotta panels as at Bishnupur, mud walls with white alpona, thatch, bamboo, *patachitra* scrolls, and *dokra* metal figures.
- **River ghat** (Nodi Ghat / Nodir Alo): broad steps down to the water, a small Shiva shrine, wooden country boats (*nouka*), and a lamp-lit edge. Keep the area clear of immersion imagery (no broken idol frames in the water).
- **Kumortuli-inspired details** (Smritir Shohor and Bonedi Para): straw-and-bamboo kathamo armatures, unpainted clay figures under plastic sheets, and narrow studio lanes. **Show unfinished idols respectfully**: in the real workshop context, eyes are unpainted until chokkhudan.

### Street props
- Phuchka cart with a big round basket of shells, a steel pot of tamarind water, and a leaf bowl (*shaal pata*). Jhalmuri vendor with a tin canister and paper cones. Kathi roll stall with a flat tawa and a menu board. Moglai porota pan.
- Cha stall with a kettle on a coal *unun* (stove), stacks of **bhNar**, and glass jars of biscuits.
- Mishti shop counter with trays of white roshogolla, sondesh in shapes, and mishti doi in clay pots (*bhNar*/*hNari*).
- Balloon sellers, toy vendors (bamboo flutes, plastic trumpets, spinning lights), and **bangle stalls**.
- Volunteer desk with a megaphone, banner, rope barriers, and "Entry / Exit" (প্রবেশ / প্রস্থান) signs; a police kiosk; a drinking-water stall.
- Dhaki with a white feather plume on the dhak, and a boy with a kansor.
- Dhunuchi pots with smoke VFX; brass lamps (*prodip*); conch shells; *ghot* (pot) with mango leaves; banana plants at the entrance; marigold garlands.
- Loudspeaker cones on bamboo poles, tangled wiring, generator boxes, strings of bulbs, and big printed sponsor-style banners using fictional brands.
- Hand-pulled rickshaws, yellow taxis, autos, and cycles parked along the lane edge.

### Lighting and mood
- Warm, low key light from bulbs and lamps, with cool indigo ambient fill. Haze from dhunuchi smoke and street-food steam. Bloom on bulb arches.
- Light the idol **from the front and slightly above**, keeping the face clear and luminous, which matches real pandal practice.
- The finale shifts the grade from indigo to peach and gold at the ghat, with mist on the river and fading bulbs. Calm, with no procession.
